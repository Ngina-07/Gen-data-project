MALARIA INCIDENCE ANALYSIS IN KENYA (2015–2025)

Project Overview

This project analyzes malaria incidence in Kenya from 2015 to 2025 to understand trends over time, seasonal patterns, differences across counties, and the relationship between rainfall and malaria incidence.

1. What problem are you solving?

Malaria is a major public health concern in Kenya. Understanding how malaria incidence changes over time and across different counties is important for effective prevention and control.

My project addresses the need for better understanding of malaria patterns in Kenya by analyzing annual trends, monthly variations, county-level differences, and the relationship between rainfall and malaria incidence.

The aim is to provide useful information that can support malaria prevention, public health planning, and resource allocation.

2. What tools did you use?

I used four main data analytics tools: Excel, SQL, Python, and Tableau.

* Excel: Used for data inspection, organization, and preliminary analysis.
* SQL: Used to query, filter, and summarize the data.
* Python: Used for data cleaning, exploratory data analysis, and statistical analysis.
* Tableau: Used to create visualizations and an interactive dashboard showing malaria trends, county comparisons, and rainfall patterns.

These tools helped me transform raw data into meaningful insights that can be understood and used for decision-making.

3. What insights did you discover, and what solutions do you offer?

My project investigates how malaria incidence has changed over time, which months recorded the highest and lowest average incidence, which counties recorded the highest and lowest average incidence, and whether rainfall is related to malaria incidence.

One of my findings was that Homa Bay recorded the highest average malaria incidence among the counties in my analysis.

This finding highlights the importance of identifying areas that may require greater attention in malaria prevention and control.

The project recommends using malaria trends to identify high-risk periods, prioritizing prevention efforts in counties with high incidence, and considering rainfall patterns when planning malaria control activities.

The main insight is that malaria incidence varies across different areas and periods. Understanding these differences can help public health stakeholders make more informed decisions about prevention and resource allocation.

4. How would a business or community benefit from your work?

My project has potential social impact because it provides information that can support malaria prevention and public health planning in Kenya.

Health authorities can use the findings to identify areas that may need additional resources, while communities can benefit from improved awareness of malaria risks and prevention measures.

The analysis can also help public health organizations plan prevention campaigns and monitor changes in malaria incidence over time.

Ultimately, my project demonstrates how data analytics can turn raw malaria data into meaningful information that supports better public health decisions.

Conclusion

My project demonstrates how data analytics can be used to understand malaria patterns in Kenya, identify high-risk areas, and support evidence-based public health decisions.

By combining Excel, SQL, Python, and Tableau, I transformed raw data into meaningful insights that can contribute to more effective malaria prevention and control.

The findings provide a foundation for understanding malaria trends and identifying areas where further investigation and prevention efforts may be needed.
